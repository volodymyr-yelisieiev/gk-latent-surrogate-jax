\chapter{Experimental Setup}
\label{chap:experiments}

\section{Evidence Tiers}

Experiments are divided into three evidence tiers. Synthetic smoke runs verify that the complete
pipeline executes without private data or GPU hardware. Tiny real-data and small multi-trajectory
runs validate the Cyclone schema, diagnostic availability, and held-out evaluation commands. The
medium experiment is the only tier used for the principal sequence-model comparison. This
separation avoids presenting a debugging fixture as scientific evidence.

\section{Medium Dataset}

The medium latent cache contains 51 trajectories and 1,173 snapshots after preprocessing. Each
trajectory contributes 23 retained time steps. The input snapshot has four channels and spatial
shape $(32,8,16,85,32)$. The associated targets are a scalar flux and two 32-bin spectra. Data are
read from the preprocessed Cyclone/KvikIO layout with offset 80 and temporal subsampling by eight.

The deterministic trajectory split uses seed 52. It assigns 41 trajectories to training, five to
validation, and five to test. This gives 943 training, 115 validation, and 115 test latent points.
With context length eight and one-step training targets, the cache exposes 615 training windows and
75 windows in each held-out split. The eight-step test rollout requires longer windows and yields
40 evaluated windows across the five test trajectories.

\section{Encoder Training and Validation}

The 128-dimensional encoder is trained for 500 update steps with a global batch size of four across
four GPUs. The optimizer uses learning rate $10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$, 25 warmup steps, and
gradient clipping. Diagnostic and SimSiam weights are given in \autoref{chap:method}.

After embedding the dataset, a ridge regressor is fitted from frozen training latents to flux and
evaluated on the validation trajectories. This probe asks whether flux information is linearly
accessible without changing the encoder. PCA and t-SNE are also computed for qualitative
inspection. These projections are descriptive; they are not used to select the sequence model and
cannot by themselves prove that the representation captures physical state.

\section{Sequence Comparison}

The principal comparison uses persistence, GRU, causal Transformer, cache-normalized GRU, and
cache-normalized causal Transformer. Each learned model has its own checkpoint, but the encoder,
latent cache, validation trajectories, context length, forecast horizon, diagnostic heads, and
aggregation are fixed. The four learned candidates and persistence are first evaluated on the
validation split. Sequence checkpoints are selected by validation latent RMSE, then flux RMSE
selects the strongest learned family; that candidate and the
predeclared persistence reference are then compared on the test manifest. Because the manifest was
inspected in more than one retained evaluation and is known during this audit, the result is
retrospective rather than a prospective locked test. The test comparison is
summarized in \autoref{tab:main-results}.

All learned sequence models train for 500 steps. The GRU has one 256-unit layer. The Transformer
has width 256, depth three, four heads, and no dropout. Cache-normalized variants calculate
per-latent statistics on training trajectories and use the same architecture and optimization
schedule as their unnormalized counterparts. GRU runs use training seed 53 and Transformer runs use
seed 54; consequently, architecture and initialization are not fully disentangled. ``Best learned
candidate'' therefore means the lowest observed validation RMSE among these single-seed runs.

An integrity audit found that an earlier exploratory comparison had combined an encoder/cache made
with split seed 52 and downstream evaluations made with different split seeds. Four of five nominal
test trajectories in one such evaluation had therefore been visible during encoder training. Those
numbers are excluded from empirical evidence. The retained experiment uses trajectory-split seed 52
for encoder training, cache construction, sequence training, validation selection, and test
evaluation, with stored trajectory-manifest hashes checked at the pipeline boundaries. The separate
optimization seeds remain 53 for GRU runs and 54 for Transformer runs as stated above.

\section{Metrics}

Flux RMSE is the primary metric because the research question concerns transport-predictive
rollouts. Flux MAE and relative error provide complementary scale information. Latent MSE measures
representation-space trajectory accuracy. Spectra relative $L_2$, log-MSE, and shape correlation
are retained as secondary diagnostics. Stability requires finite outputs over the complete rollout.

Metrics are computed per horizon and aggregated with equal trajectory weights. This matters because
overlapping windows are correlated and the number of valid windows can differ across trajectories.
The headline flux RMSE is the square root of the mean trajectory MSE across trajectories and
horizons. It is not the arithmetic mean of the five per-trajectory RMSE values. The paired analysis
uses those five per-trajectory RMSE values and therefore targets a different estimand. Overlapping
windows are not treated as independent physical realizations. Its 95\% t interval is descriptive,
contains no training-seed uncertainty, and is not used for a significance claim. Flux errors are
reported in preprocessed target units because the retained artifact does not establish a physical
unit.

\section{Additional Experiments}

No transfer-learning, log-spectra sensitivity, or external-architecture result is included in the
evidence set. These branches remain implementation options, but their data and initialization
protocols are not matched to the principal comparison and no numerical claim is made from them.

GyroSwin remains related work rather than an experimental row. Public architecture descriptions do
not provide the matched artifact bundle needed to run its checkpoint under the exact split and
metric implementation used here.
