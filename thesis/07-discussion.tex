\chapter{Discussion}
\label{chap:discussion}

\section{Answer to the Research Question}

The frozen retrospective five-fold nested group cross-validation answers the central question
negatively for the declared setting. The selected learned model has flux RMSE 14.6729, while direct
persistence of the observed flux has 10.0207. The learned-minus-observed difference is +4.6522 and
its 95\% hierarchical bootstrap interval [+2.5788, +6.6470] excludes zero. The correct statement
is therefore not that the latent surrogate is useless in general, but that this implementation,
representation, training budget, and horizon do not improve the strong observed-diagnostic
baseline.

The architecture result is also more modest than a conventional leaderboard. Transformer is
selected in three folds and GRU in two. The family choice is made from validation means over five
matched seeds, while sequence checkpoints are chosen from validation latent error. This nesting
prevents a test-aware choice, but it also means that a single global ranking would misrepresent the
evidence. The study demonstrates a reproducible selection procedure rather than a universally best
temporal architecture.

\section{What the Baselines Reveal}

Observed-flux persistence is a demanding baseline because the diagnostic can be slowly varying
over the short forecast horizon. A learned latent rollout must first preserve the relevant state,
then evolve it accurately, and finally decode it through the frozen head. The aggregate latent
rollout error is consequently a compound quantity, not a pure measure of sequence-model quality.

Decoded latent persistence has flux RMSE 14.8049, nearly matching the learned result. This suggests
that the temporal models did not create a clear short-horizon diagnostic advantage over simply
holding the encoded state fixed. The latent-MSE secondary estimate is similarly inconclusive:
the learned-minus-persistence difference is +0.0189 with interval [-0.0699,+0.1332]. A reviewer
should not convert that interval into evidence for either improvement or degradation.

The diagnostic-head oracle reaches 12.3732 when given true future latents. Its improvement over
the learned rollout shows that the rollout and the representation-to-diagnostic path both leave
room for error. Because the oracle remains worse than observed persistence, it cannot identify the
head as the sole bottleneck or provide an upper bound on a future model. It is best interpreted as
an analysis control: even perfect latent access under the current head would not answer the
research question positively.

\section{Variation Across Folds and Seeds}

The primary difference is positive in every outer fold, with point estimates from +1.4189 to
+7.2051. This consistency is stronger than a result driven by one unlucky split, while the range
also shows that the magnitude is regime-dependent. The hierarchically weighted fraction of
trajectory runs improved by the learned model is 31.6\%; this is below one half and is reported
with the weighting rule rather than as an unqualified raw-row proportion.

Seed variability is not hidden in a single mean. Across folds, the mean learned-minus-observed
difference ranges from 3.8611 for seed 53 to 5.0306 for seed 54; the across-fold standard
deviations range from 2.1007 to 2.5897. Every seed has a positive mean difference. These summaries
support the direction of the negative result, but they do not replace independent trajectories or
additional physical regimes.

\section{Spectra and Representation Quality}

Both spectral targets favour observed persistence by a wide margin in relative error and shape
correlation. The learned $\code{fluxspec}$ relative $L_2$ is 1.5026 versus 0.4433 for observed
persistence, and the learned $\code{kyspec}$ relative $L_2$ is 28.1003 versus 0.5296. The shape
correlations are 0.6011 and 0.7226 for the learned model, compared with 0.9123 and 0.9330 for the
observed baseline. These values are not a single physical-fidelity score because the two spectra
have different scales, but their direction agrees with the flux result.

The representation should therefore be described as diagnostic-oriented rather than physically
complete. A 128-dimensional state can retain enough signal for finite forecasts and nontrivial
shape correlations while still discarding scale information needed for quantitative transport
prediction. The absence of a decoder also limits what can be claimed: no result here establishes
that the latent vector can reconstruct the full five-dimensional distribution.

\section{Methodological Contribution}

The most important contribution is the auditability of the negative result. The pipeline binds
trajectory manifests to encoders, caches, checkpoints, and evaluators; it fits normalization on
training trajectories; it records resolved configuration hashes; and it makes the validation/test
barrier explicit. The stage ledger distinguishes accepted stages from the 25 unselected-family
test stages, so the absence of unselected test metrics is itself verifiable. W\&B is explicitly
disabled rather than silently absent, and the release manifest contains no private paths or
trajectory identifiers.

This process also corrected a substantive failure mode. Earlier exploratory runs mixed trajectory
split seeds between representation learning and downstream evaluation. Such a run can produce
plausible numbers while allowing the encoder to see trajectories later called test. Removing those
numbers, rather than selecting the most favourable surviving value, is necessary for a defensible
thesis. The current result is weaker than the withdrawn headlines but scientifically stronger.

\section{Limitations}

The conclusions are bounded in several ways:

\begin{itemize}
  \item The 51 trajectories come from one preprocessed Cyclone/KvikIO universe. The outer folds
        are disjoint groups, but they do not represent independent machines, regimes, or
        preprocessing pipelines.
  \item Nested cross-validation is retrospective because the universe and manifest were available
        during development. It estimates performance on this universe; it is not a pristine final
        test set.
  \item The forecast context and horizon are short. Eight recursive steps do not establish
        long-term stability, saturation, or useful speedup relative to the gyrokinetic solver.
  \item The encoder uses aggressive subsampling and a compact 128-dimensional state. Spatial
        information that is important for transport may be discarded before temporal modelling.
  \item The supervised diagnostic heads are frozen for rollout evaluation. The oracle control
        demonstrates a head/representation gap but cannot distinguish the contributions of the
        head, encoder, and temporal model.
  \item Relative spectral errors are sensitive to target norms, and the two spectra have different
        scales. They are reported separately and do not justify a pooled physical claim.
  \item Raw data, caches, checkpoints, and per-trajectory rows remain on the authorized server.
        The repository provides the code, manifests, hashes, synthetic fallback, and sanitized
        release, but not a self-contained raw-data reproduction.
  \item No matched GyroSwin checkpoint, full-field decoder, physical-unit calibration, or end-to-end
        runtime benchmark was available. None is inferred from this experiment.
\end{itemize}

\section{Future Work}

The first priority is not a larger temporal architecture. A follow-up should test whether a less
aggressive spatial encoder, patch mixing, or a decoder improves the diagnostic oracle while
preserving the same nested split discipline. A second priority is to normalize the two spectral
targets separately so that the larger-scale target cannot dominate the representation objective.
Only after those changes should context length, horizon, and sequence capacity be expanded.

If an unseen set of at least ten trajectories becomes available, it should be frozen before any
new architecture or budget is selected and evaluated once at the end. Otherwise the same nested
group protocol should be repeated on a new universe revision, with its own protocol ID and source
tag. A matched external benchmark would require the same data revision, temporal sampling,
normalization, horizon, diagnostic definitions, and aggregation; without that bundle, a numerical
comparison with GyroSwin would be misleading.
