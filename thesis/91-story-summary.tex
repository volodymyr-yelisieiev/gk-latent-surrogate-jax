\chapter{Story Summary}
\label{app:story-summary}

This appendix follows the Institute for Machine Learning thesis requirement. Each answer is kept
within three sentences.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{What is the central question?}

  Can a compact JAX/Flax latent surrogate learn short-horizon dynamics from preprocessed
  five-dimensional gyrokinetic snapshots, and can a learned sequence model improve flux-predictive
  rollout RMSE over persistence on unseen trajectories?

  \item \textbf{Why is this question important?}

  Nonlinear gyrokinetic simulations are computationally expensive because they evolve a
  five-dimensional distribution function. A compact diagnostic-preserving state could make
  temporal modelling cheaper without claiming complete field reconstruction.

  \item \textbf{What evidence/data (variables) are needed to answer this question?}

  The evidence consists of time-ordered five-dimensional snapshots, scalar flux, two
  one-dimensional spectra, trajectory identifiers, and held-out rollout windows. Encoder, cache,
  split seed, context, horizon, model-selection rule, and aggregation metadata must also match.

  \item \textbf{What methods are used to get this evidence/data?}

  A Cyclone/KvikIO adapter validates a channel-first tensor contract, and a convolutional encoder
  trained with SimSiam and diagnostic losses produces 128-dimensional latents. Sequence windows are
  split by trajectory, and artifact lineage is checked before training or evaluation.

  \item \textbf{What analyses must be applied to the data to answer the central question?}

  Persistence, GRU, and Transformer candidates are evaluated on validation, where the learned model
  is selected by flux RMSE. Only that fixed candidate and persistence are then rolled out for eight
  steps on the retrospective test trajectories with trajectory-balanced metrics.

  \item \textbf{What evidence/data (values for the variables) were obtained?}

  The medium cache contains 51 trajectories and 1,173 latent vectors, split 41/5/5 into
  train/validation/test with seed 52. The test contains 40 eight-step windows from five
  trajectories that were not used for encoder or sequence training.

  \item \textbf{What were the results of the analyses?}

  The validation-selected cache-normalized Transformer obtains test flux RMSE 11.4255 and latent
  MSE 0.0738. Persistence obtains flux RMSE 11.9889 and latent MSE 0.1019; the Transformer improves
  per-trajectory flux RMSE in three of five cases.

  \item \textbf{How did the analyses answer the central question?}

  The learned model has a 4.70\% lower primary flux-RMSE point estimate than persistence in this
  retrospective realization. The paired interval includes zero, so the result does not establish
  significance, repeatability, or broader superiority.

  \item \textbf{What does this answer tell us about the broader field?}

  Complex latent dynamics should not be assumed to beat a strong persistence baseline, especially
  for slowly changing states. Strict split lineage, validation-only selection, and honest reporting
  of bounded results are necessary before drawing broader conclusions about neural surrogates.
\end{enumerate}
