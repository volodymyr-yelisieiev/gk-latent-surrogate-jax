\chapter{Introduction}

\section{Motivation}

Magnetically confined fusion plasmas exhibit turbulence that transports heat and particles
across magnetic surfaces. Predicting this transport is important for understanding confinement,
but the nonlinear gyrokinetic equations evolve a five-dimensional distribution function and are
expensive to solve at high fidelity. The dimensionality is not incidental: it preserves kinetic
effects that reduced transport models may omit \citep{abel2013multiscale,paischer2025gyroswin}.

Machine-learning surrogates offer a complementary approximation path. A full-field surrogate can
directly predict the next high-dimensional state, but doing so requires a model and loss over every
field value. This thesis studies a narrower question: can the state first be compressed into a
small vector whose dynamics are easier to learn, while diagnostic heads preserve information about
quantities of physical interest? The proposed pipeline therefore separates spatial representation
learning from temporal prediction. It maps each five-dimensional snapshot $x_t$ to a latent vector
$z_t$, evolves $z_t$ autoregressively, and evaluates the rollout both in latent space and through
diagnostic predictions of flux and spectra.

This separation has three practical attractions. First, sequence models operate on vectors rather
than the full spatial grid. Second, diagnostic errors can be evaluated without claiming that a
latent vector reconstructs the complete distribution function. Third, simple temporal baselines
can be compared under an identical cached representation. The trade-off is equally important: the
surrogate is useful only for the diagnostic quantities explicitly retained by the representation,
and it cannot be interpreted as a full simulation replacement.

\section{Research Question}

The central research question is:

\begin{quote}
Can a compact JAX/Flax latent surrogate learn short-horizon gyrokinetic dynamics from
preprocessed five-dimensional field snapshots, and do latent sequence models improve
flux-predictive rollouts over a persistence baseline on held-out trajectories?
\end{quote}

The question is operationalized through three sub-questions:

\begin{enumerate}[label=RQ\arabic*]
  \item Does the encoder retain flux-relevant information in a 128-dimensional latent space?
  \item Which of persistence, a gated recurrent unit (GRU), and a causal Transformer produces the
        best eight-step latent rollout under a common test protocol?
  \item Does cache normalization materially change the ranking of the learned sequence models?
\end{enumerate}

Flux RMSE is the primary model-selection metric. Latent mean squared error (MSE), spectra errors,
and finite-output stability are secondary diagnostics. This priority follows the scientific goal:
a numerically small latent error is not sufficient if it fails to preserve transport information.

\section{Contributions}

This thesis makes the following contributions:

\begin{itemize}
  \item a portable JAX/Flax implementation of a complete latent-surrogate workflow, including
        synthetic fixtures and a real Cyclone/KvikIO data adapter;
  \item an explicit channel-first tensor contract for five spatial dimensions and deterministic
        trajectory-level train, validation, and test splits;
  \item a representation objective combining SimSiam-style invariance with supervised flux and
        spectra heads;
  \item latent caching and comparable persistence, GRU, MLP-delta, and causal Transformer dynamics;
  \item trajectory-balanced rollout evaluation with latent, flux, spectra, and stability metrics;
  \item a leakage-resistant medium-scale comparison with validation-only model selection and a
        locked test, showing a bounded 4.70\% flux-RMSE point-estimate improvement for the selected
        Transformer over persistence; and
  \item a reproducibility-oriented command-line interface, configuration system, test suite, and
        provenance ledger that separates engineering smoke tests from scientific evidence.
\end{itemize}

\section{Scope and Claim Boundaries}

The work predicts latent vectors and diagnostics; it does not reconstruct the complete
five-dimensional state. The medium experiment covers 51 trajectories from one preprocessed data
source, split at trajectory level into 41 training, five validation, and five test trajectories
with one canonical seed. Generalization to other regimes, machines, preprocessing choices, or
substantially longer horizons is not established.

GyroSwin is discussed as related work because it targets scalable full-field five-dimensional
surrogacy \citep{paischer2025gyroswin}. A direct numeric comparison is intentionally omitted. Such
a comparison would require the same dataset version, trajectory split, temporal sampling,
forecast horizon, target definition, normalization, and metric implementation. Those matching
materials were unavailable for the present experiment.
