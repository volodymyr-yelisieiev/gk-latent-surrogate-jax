\section{Protocol Integrity Audit}

Before final model assessment, the retained experiment lineage was reconstructed from resolved
configs, encoder metadata, latent-cache metadata, and trajectory identifiers. This audit found that
the encoder and medium cache used trajectory-split seed 52, while historical downstream runs used
seeds 53 or 54. Four of the five trajectories labelled as test under each downstream seed had
therefore occurred in the seed-52 encoder training split. The historical headline table, including
the previously reported flux RMSE 9.2270, is not an end-to-end held-out result and is excluded from
the scientific conclusions.

The corrected implementation records the selected trajectory identifiers, a SHA-256 manifest of
their ordering, the data-split seed, training seed, upstream cache and checkpoint paths, horizon,
and aggregation rule. It also rejects an encoder, cache, or sequence checkpoint whose recorded
split seed differs from the evaluation configuration. All results below use data-split seed 52 from
representation learning through evaluation. Validation and test manifests differ, while every row
within a split has the same manifest.

\section{Representation Inspection}

\autoref{fig:representation} shows descriptive projections of the 1,173 cached latent vectors.
Colour variation indicates association with flux, but neither PCA nor t-SNE is used as proof of
prediction quality. Under the corrected seed-52 split, a ridge head fitted only on 943 frozen
training latents obtains validation flux RMSE 13.1779 on 115 held-out latent points. This answers
RQ1 quantitatively: flux is linearly accessible from the representation under the canonical split,
without establishing that the representation is sufficient for temporal prediction.

\section{Validation-Based Model Selection}

The model family and normalization choice are fixed using the seed-52 validation split before the
test split is inspected. Each validation row uses five trajectories, 40 windows, an eight-step
recursive horizon, and trajectory-balanced aggregation. Flux RMSE is the predeclared primary
selection metric.

  \centering
  \caption{Seed-52 validation results used for model selection. Lower is better. The bold row is
  the selected learned model; persistence is a parameter-free reference rather than a candidate
  architecture.}
  \label{tab:validation-results}
  \scriptsize
  \setlength{\tabcolsep}{3.5pt}
  \begin{tabular}{lrrrrc}
    \toprule
    Model & Latent MSE & Flux RMSE & Flux MAE & Flux rel. error & Stable \\
    \midrule
    Persistence & 0.4341 & 17.7557 & 12.7683 & 0.2655 & yes \\
    GRU & 0.6144 & 19.9275 & 14.3363 & 0.2905 & yes \\
    Transformer & 0.4823 & 18.6828 & 14.0105 & 0.2885 & yes \\
    GRU, cache-normalized & 0.5478 & 19.4800 & 14.7968 & 0.3112 & yes \\
    \textbf{Transformer, cache-normalized} & \textbf{0.4560} & \textbf{18.1793} &
      \textbf{12.9650} & \textbf{0.2664} & yes \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

The cache-normalized Transformer is the best learned candidate on validation flux RMSE. It does not
beat persistence on that split, but it is selected without access to the test outcomes because the
research question concerns the strongest learned sequence model. GRU variants are not carried
forward. This separation prevents choosing a model after observing multiple test rows.

Cache normalization lowers validation flux RMSE from 19.9275 to 19.4800 for the GRU and from
18.6828 to 18.1793 for the Transformer. It improves both learned point estimates without changing
their ranking: the Transformer remains ahead of the GRU, and persistence remains ahead of every
learned candidate. RQ3 is therefore answered as a consistent improvement in the two point estimates,
but not as a ranking change or a replicated effect.

\section{Locked Test Comparison}

After selection, only the fixed cache-normalized Transformer and the predeclared persistence
baseline are evaluated on the seed-52 test split. Both rows use the same five test trajectories,
40 rollout windows, eight forecast steps, and the same stored trajectory manifest (SHA-256 prefix
\texttt{519a2b02}).

  \centering
  \caption{Locked seed-52 test comparison. Flux RMSE is the primary metric; lower is better for all
  displayed errors.}

  \begin{tabular}{lrrrrrc}
    \toprule
    Model & Latent MSE & Flux RMSE & Flux MAE & Flux rel. error & Spectra rel. $L_2$ & Stable \\
    \midrule

    Transformer (cache norm.) & \textbf{0.0738} & \textbf{11.4255} & \textbf{10.2571} &
      \textbf{2.2083} & \textbf{19.1686} & yes \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

The selected Transformer improves the primary test point estimate: its flux RMSE is 4.70\% lower
than persistence. It also reduces flux MAE by 4.69\%, latent MSE by 27.55\%, and aggregate spectra
relative $L_2$ by 1.83\%. Per-trajectory flux RMSE improves in three of five cases. The paired mean
difference (Transformer minus persistence) is $-0.8279$ with a descriptive 95\% t interval
$[-2.2249,0.5692]$; because the interval includes zero and only one training seed is used, this is
bounded evidence for the fixed protocol rather than a significance or equivalence claim.

\section{Spectral Diagnostics}

The selected Transformer yields aggregate spectra relative $L_2$ 19.1686 compared with 19.5257 for
persistence. The value is dominated by the \code{kyspec} component (35.2386 versus 35.9227), while
\code{fluxspec} relative errors are 3.0987 and 3.1287. Aggregate shape correlations are nearly
identical (0.53439 versus 0.53419), so the relative-error change should not be interpreted as a
general improvement in spectral structure. The large relative values also show that scale-sensitive
spectral prediction remains a weakness of both approaches.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.68\textwidth]{verified_spectra_mse_by_step.png}
  \caption{Aggregate spectra MSE by forecast horizon for the selected Transformer and persistence
  on the locked seed-52 test split. The two spectra have different numerical scales, so the curves must be read
  together with per-spectrum relative and shape metrics.}
  \label{fig:spectra-horizon}
\end{figure}

Historical log-spectra runs with mixed representation/downstream splits are retained only as
invalidated engineering records. The language-model-transfer test is a separate, internally
seed-consistent seed-62 protocol, but it has no matched persistence row or validation-based model
selection under the accepted seed-52 cache. It is therefore excluded from the principal comparison
without being described as split-leaked.
