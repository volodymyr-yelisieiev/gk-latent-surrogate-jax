\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{chap:conclusion}

This thesis developed and audited a compact latent time-series surrogate for preprocessed
five-dimensional gyrokinetic turbulence snapshots. The JAX/Flax pipeline covers data inspection,
conservative augmentation, joint representation and diagnostic training, latent caching, multiple
temporal models, recursive rollout, trajectory-balanced metrics, and artifact provenance. A
synthetic fallback and extensive verification suite make the software testable without private data
or GPU hardware.

The final experiment uses one canonical trajectory split from representation learning through
evaluation. The cache-normalized Transformer was selected as the best learned model on validation
flux RMSE and then evaluated once on a locked test split. It obtains flux RMSE 11.4255, compared with
11.9889 for persistence, reducing the primary point estimate by 4.70\%. Flux MAE, latent MSE, and
spectra relative error also decrease. The model improves per-trajectory flux RMSE in three of five
cases, but the paired interval includes zero; the experiment therefore supports a bounded
single-protocol improvement rather than a significance or general-superiority claim.

The result is bounded but useful. It shows that the selected representation and training budget can
improve on a strong temporal baseline in one locked comparison, and it identifies encoder fidelity,
repeated matched seeds, and strict validation-only selection as priorities for
future work. The thesis makes no claim about full-field reconstruction, long-term forecasting,
broad gyrokinetic generalization, solver speedup, or superiority to GyroSwin. Its main contribution
is a reproducible and leakage-resistant experimental foundation on which those questions can be
tested credibly.
