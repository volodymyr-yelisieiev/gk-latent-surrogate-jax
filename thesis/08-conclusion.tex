\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{chap:conclusion}

This thesis investigated whether a compact latent representation of preprocessed five-dimensional
gyrokinetic snapshots could support useful short-horizon forecasts of transport diagnostics. The
JAX/Flax workflow encodes each snapshot as a 128-dimensional state and caches the resulting
trajectory representations. It then trains gated recurrent unit (GRU) and causal Transformer
sequence models and evaluates eight recursive steps through frozen flux and spectral diagnostic
heads. The accepted evaluation covers 51 trajectories and uses retrospective five-fold nested group
cross-validation with matched training seeds 52, 53, 54, 55, and 56. Because the universe and
protocol were available during development, this is not a pristine locked-test result.

The evidence gives a qualified answer to RQ1. The representation retains usable diagnostic
information, but not enough for accurate diagnostic rollout relative to observed persistence. When
supplied with true future latent states, the diagnostic-head oracle reaches flux RMSE 12.3732,
compared with 10.0207 for direct persistence of the observed flux. The oracle therefore exposes a
gap in the representation-to-diagnostic path, but it does not determine how much of that gap comes
from the encoder or diagnostic head, nor does it isolate one causal bottleneck.

RQ2 has a clear negative answer under the declared protocol. The selected learned sequence models
have flux RMSE 14.6729, versus 10.0207 for direct observed-flux persistence. The
learned-minus-persistence difference is +4.6522, with a paired hierarchical bootstrap interval of
[+2.5788, +6.6470]. The hierarchically weighted fraction of improved paired runs is 31.6\%.
Because the primary interval is strictly positive, the experiment supports a bounded negative
result: on this universe and over this eight-step horizon, the selected learned models are less
accurate than the baseline. This finding does not imply that latent sequence models are unsuitable
in other settings.

RQ3 yields no global GRU-versus-Transformer winner. Validation-only selection chose Transformer in
outer folds 0, 1, and 3 and GRU in folds 2 and 4. The preferred family changes with the split, so a
universal architecture ranking would go beyond the evidence.

The secondary comparison does not overturn the primary result. Holding the last encoded state
constant and decoding it gives flux RMSE 14.8049. Learned latent MSE is 0.4497, compared with
0.4307 for this latent-persistence reference; the difference is +0.0189 with interval
[-0.0699, +0.1332]. Since the interval includes zero, it does not establish a latent-dynamics
advantage. Together, latent persistence and the oracle show that temporal prediction and the
representation-to-diagnostic path both warrant further study, while neither control identifies a
single cause of the observed deficit.

This negative performance result is separate from the engineering and reproducibility contribution.
The repository provides a portable, testable workflow for trajectory-level splitting,
representation learning, latent caching, validation-only family selection, recursive rollout,
paired hierarchical aggregation, and artifact lineage. There are 230 accepted protocol slots and
25 deliberately skipped unselected-family test slots. W\&B was disabled by protocol; local stage
evidence and the frozen release manifest, rather than an online dashboard, define the accepted
record. These controls make the selection barrier auditable and ensure that outer-test evidence does
not determine the reported family choice.

The practical implication is methodological: a finite or visually plausible rollout is not
sufficient evidence of surrogate success. It must improve on a meaningful diagnostic baseline under
a procedure that prevents trajectory leakage and keeps validation separate from outer testing.
Scientifically, the oracle makes the spatial representation and diagnostic decoding natural first
targets for follow-up, but does not prove that either is the sole bottleneck. A subsequent study
should first test improvements to these components and then repeat the comparison under a separately
frozen protocol on a new trajectory universe or a genuinely unseen test set. Context length,
forecast horizon, and temporal model capacity should be expanded only after that comparison
establishes an advantage over observed persistence.

The result remains bounded by a single preprocessed universe of 51 trajectories, retrospective
nested cross-validation, and an eight-step rollout. The diagnostics are evaluated in preprocessed
target units, and no decoder was trained for the complete five-dimensional field. Accordingly, the
thesis does not establish full-field reconstruction, long-horizon stability, or physical-unit
accuracy. It also does not establish replacement of or speedup over a gyrokinetic solver, broad
gyrokinetic generalization, or superiority over observed persistence. No matched numerical
comparison with GyroSwin or universal superiority of either GRU or Transformer follows from this
experiment. Until a learned model consistently improves on observed persistence under a new frozen
protocol, direct persistence remains the appropriate diagnostic reference.
