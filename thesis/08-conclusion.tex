\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{chap:conclusion}

This thesis developed and audited a compact latent time-series surrogate for preprocessed
five-dimensional gyrokinetic turbulence snapshots. The JAX/Flax pipeline covers data inspection,
conservative augmentation, joint representation and diagnostic training, latent caching, multiple
temporal models, recursive rollout, trajectory-balanced metrics, and artifact provenance. A
synthetic fallback and extensive verification suite make the software testable without private data
or GPU hardware.

The final experiment uses one canonical trajectory split from representation learning through
evaluation. The cache-normalized Transformer was selected as the best learned model on validation
flux RMSE and then evaluated on a test split that is now reported retrospectively. It obtains flux
RMSE 11.4255, compared with
11.9889 for persistence, reducing the primary point estimate by 4.70\%. Flux MAE, latent MSE, and
aggregate spectra relative error also decrease, although the spectral shape correlation is
essentially unchanged and the relative errors are large. The model improves per-trajectory flux RMSE in three of five
cases, but the paired interval includes zero; the experiment therefore provides a
single-realization point estimate rather than a significance, repeatability, or
general-superiority claim.

The selected representation and training budget produced a lower point estimate than a strong
temporal baseline in this comparison. Matched seeds, an unseen test set or nested group
cross-validation, and strict validation-only selection are required before making a broader model
claim. The thesis makes no claim about full-field reconstruction, long-term forecasting, broad
gyrokinetic generalization, solver speedup, or superiority to GyroSwin. Its main contribution is
the implemented pipeline, its lineage checks, and a documented account of where the present
evidence remains weak.
