\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{chap:conclusion}

This thesis examined whether a compact latent representation of preprocessed five-dimensional
gyrokinetic snapshots can support accurate short-horizon forecasts of transport diagnostics. The
implemented JAX/Flax workflow maps each snapshot to a 128-dimensional state, caches complete
trajectory representations, and trains gated recurrent unit (GRU) and causal Transformer sequence
models. Their predictions are evaluated through frozen flux and spectral diagnostic heads over an
eight-step recursive rollout. The accepted study covers 51 trajectories and uses retrospective
five-fold nested group cross-validation with matched training seeds 52, 53, 54, 55, and 56. Since
the universe and protocol were available during development, the result is not a pristine
locked-test estimate.

For RQ1, the answer is qualified. The representation retains usable diagnostic information, but not
enough for accurate diagnostic rollout relative to observed persistence. Supplying the frozen
diagnostic head with true future latent states yields an oracle flux RMSE of 12.3732, compared with
10.0207 for direct persistence of the observed flux. Diagnostic signal therefore survives the
compression, yet the current representation-to-diagnostic path is not accurate enough to match the
primary baseline. The oracle does not distinguish information lost by the encoder from error in the
diagnostic head, and it does not isolate one causal bottleneck.

RQ2 is answered negatively under the declared protocol. The selected learned sequence models have
flux RMSE 14.6729, whereas copying the last observed flux across the forecast horizon gives 10.0207.
The learned-minus-persistence difference is +4.6522, with a paired hierarchical-bootstrap interval
of [+2.5788, +6.6470]. The learned model has lower error in 31.6\% of hierarchically weighted
paired runs. Because the primary interval is strictly positive, the experiment supports a bounded
negative result: on this universe and over this horizon, the selected learned models are less
accurate than observed-flux persistence. This finding is specific to the declared representation,
training budget, selection procedure, and data universe; it is not a general rejection of latent
sequence modelling.

RQ3 does not yield a supported global GRU-versus-Transformer winner. Validation-only selection chose
Transformer in outer folds 0, 1, and 3 and GRU in folds 2 and 4. The preferred family changes with
the split, so a universal architecture ranking would go beyond the evidence.

The latent-persistence control does not overturn the primary finding. Holding the last encoded state
constant and decoding it gives flux RMSE 14.8049. Learned latent MSE is 0.4497, compared with
0.4307 for latent persistence; the learned-minus-latent-persistence difference is +0.0189 with
interval [-0.0699, +0.1332]. Since this interval includes zero, it establishes neither an advantage
nor a clear disadvantage for the learned latent dynamics. The decoded-flux values are nearly equal,
while the latent-MSE interval includes zero. This comparison therefore provides no evidence of a
clear short-horizon gain over holding the encoded state fixed. Alongside the oracle, it indicates
that both temporal prediction and the representation-to-diagnostic path warrant further study,
while neither control assigns the observed deficit to a single cause.

This negative performance result is separate from the engineering and reproducibility contribution.
The repository provides a portable, testable workflow for trajectory-level splitting,
representation learning, latent caching, validation-only family selection, recursive rollout,
trajectory-balanced metrics, paired hierarchical aggregation, and artifact lineage. The frozen
stage plan contains 230 accepted protocol slots and 25 deliberately skipped unselected-family test
slots. W\&B was disabled by protocol; local stage evidence and the sanitized release manifest,
rather than an online dashboard, define the accepted record. These controls make the selection
barrier auditable and prevent outer-test evidence from determining the reported family choice.

The practical implication is methodological. A rollout that remains finite or appears smooth is not
therefore an accurate surrogate; it must improve on a meaningful diagnostic baseline under a
procedure that prevents trajectory leakage and separates validation from outer testing. Future work
should first improve the spatial representation and diagnostic decoding, then repeat the oracle and
persistence comparisons under the same split discipline. Only after that path shows an advantage
should longer contexts, wider temporal models, or substantially longer rollouts be treated as the
main priority. The revised system should be evaluated under a separately frozen protocol on a new
trajectory universe or, preferably, on a genuinely unseen test set.

The claims remain narrow. The 51 trajectories belong to one preprocessed universe, the evaluation
is retrospective, and eight recursive steps establish only short-horizon behaviour. Diagnostics are
reported in preprocessed target units, and no decoder was trained for the complete five-dimensional
field. The thesis therefore does not establish full-field reconstruction, long-horizon stability,
physical-unit accuracy, replacement of or speedup over a gyrokinetic solver, broad gyrokinetic
generalization, or superiority over observed persistence. It also provides no matched numerical
comparison with GyroSwin and no evidence that either GRU or Transformer is universally superior.
Until a learned model consistently improves on observed persistence under a new frozen protocol,
direct persistence remains the appropriate diagnostic reference.
